Reading Response: Worth Designing Beautifully
    Principle 1.16: Design is the radical synthesis of means and ends into a third type of thing--both useful and beautiful

I’d like to focus on this particular Artful Design Principle, as well as a couple other moments from the chapter for my response. A statement that really resonated with me, and I think is a crux of the whole reading, is “Anything worth designing is worth designing beautifully” (Wang, 30).

These two quotes, one a principle and one a corollary, exemplify how the useful and beautiful can be fused into one thing. For example, a pencil bag and its zipper are in fact, made of its own zipper. That quality is superfluous, but that is what gives it its character, which complements its function. The trash can handle I talked about in my etude has a similar dual-use, with an additional aesthetic purpose. Some might argue that being able to unzip the entire bag has no use, as does being able to stack multiple small trash cans on top of each other with their handles. However, the beauty and emotional appeal to me comes from knowing that someone put the effort in to combine function(s) and form.

Another quote that stood out to me was: “More than function, design is experience.” Despite the Lamborghini photo, I thought of UX. I thought about every bad UI/UX experience I’ve had in a website–icons that mean things they shouldn't, scrolljacking, buttons in unintuitive places. And I realize that a lot of designing good UI comes from having experienced what good UI is. Good UI is supposed to feel intuitive, so it makes sense that it comes from humanity’s collective experience of what good websites they’ve used in the past.

Aristotle saying YO is a design choice. The book being a graphic novel, with Ge pointing at things from speech bubbles, is a choice that makes a book full of big claims feel approachable. I read it faster and with my guard down as a result. It also acts out Principle 1.13, that design is human; like the ren character that brands the cover, where aesthetics and pragmatics lean on each other like two legs. (Wang, 47).

Ultimately, this chapter introduced both functionally and aesthetically how design is vastly intentional. Additionally, if a designer isn’t willing to put in effort and care to make a design beautiful in complement to its purpose, then it threatens the very purpose of the design itself. This notion turned on its head what I originally thought design to be–something that's primary purpose was purpose, and not personality. However, Wang makes an important point in this chapter: technology, in the name of progress, drives us to prioritize speed and sacrifice all the things that make art and design human.
