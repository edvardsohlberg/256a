Part 2

1: Stanford's main quad is beautiful to me for a number of reasons. The main one is that learning about the features and design decisions of Main Quad is a journey, still ongoing, that has only revealed to me that there is still so much I don’t know about the architecture of campus. For example, I learned from a tour guide that the columns supporting each small arch are sized to be anthropomorphic, creating the illusion that the campus is busy from a distance even if the arcades are sparsely filled. The way the quad splits campus across multiple axes, displaying the splendor of Memorial Church from palm drive, and pointing the Engineering Quad and Green Library on either side, two significant campus landmarks. There are imperfections present too, such as the palm trees, far from home, placed in the basins where they struggle to thrive in NorCal weather. Stanford’s campus stands out to me as particularly manicured compared to others, but I appreciate that there is still a lot of intentionality, humanity, and imperfection that makes itself home here.

2: I live in a one-room quad this year, and the layout of the room is both the intentional and unintentional result of 4 people sharing a living space. The one lofted bed for the person who wanted to make the most of limited space. From a poster to an ice pick, the wall items of each tenant that tell a story and add visual contrast. Makeshift shoe walls around cables are present that look messy, but are really there because one roommate decided to bring a roomba. All of these combine to create a space that is clearly well lived, but not every feature’s purpose stands out right away. Warm and human.

3. The part of the dorm room trashcan that stands out to me is the handle. I’m always satisfied when I see one part of an object or tool serve multiple purposes depending on its position–in this case, the white handle of the dorm room trash can serve as an accent at rest, a grip for transport, or a platform when stacking another module of the same can on top. It’s also fun to see that, on an individual module, the odd angled cut that would normally be bad design for a trashcan provides a nice opening to put trash in when you stack them.
